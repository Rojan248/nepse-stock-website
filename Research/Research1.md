# Quantitative Forecasting in the NEPSE Ecosystem: An "Atom of Thoughts" Analysis

## PHASE 1: INDEPENDENT ATOMIC ANALYSIS

### Atom 1: The NEPSE Baseline Reality
The Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) operates under rigid structural constraints that mechanically alter market micro-structure. Liquidity is highly fragmented; a few sectors (e.g., Commercial Banks, Hydropower, Microfinance) absorb the majority of capital, leaving a vast tail of illiquid assets. The market is overwhelmingly dominated by retail investors (often representing >75% of daily turnover), generating extreme herd behavior and emotional volatility. Structurally, it is a strictly long-only market—there is no legal short-selling and no derivatives/options market, resulting in asymmetrical downside velocity (market corrections are purely liquidations of long positions). Regulatory bodies (SEBON and CDSC) impose distinct limits, such as margin lending caps (historically the 15/20 crore limits, heavily impacting high net-worth trading), and a T+2 settlement cycle that frequently drives localized, short-term liquidity crunches. Finally, strict circuit breakers cap individual stock movements at ±10% per day, with index-wide halts triggered at 4%, 5%, and 6%, mechanically truncating natural momentum distribution.

### Atom 2: Pure Local Price Action
In the NEPSE ecosystem, isolated price-action and volume anomalies are the primary footprint of underlying business growth accumulation. Due to the lack of institutional block-trading transparency, smart money accumulation manifests as **"Dry Volume Accumulation"**—prolonged, tight sideways price action accompanied by historically low volume, signaling that weak hands have exited and the tradable float is being locked. Sudden volume spikes exceeding 300% of the 20-day Simple Moving Average (SMA), specifically clustered 10 to 15 days prior to Nepalese fiscal quarter-ends (Ashad, Ashwin, Poush, Chaitra), are highly correlated with insider anticipation of strong earnings. Chart patterns such as prolonged "W-bottoms" (double bottoms with a higher second trough) and multi-month rounding bottoms have a high historical correlation with sustainable growth, as they reflect the slow, inefficient digestion of fundamental data by the retail majority.

### Atom 3: Pure Global Forecasting Models
Viewed in a strict vacuum, advanced quantitative forecasting relies on mathematically rigorous, globally recognized models:
1. **Long Short-Term Memory Networks (LSTMs):** A recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture explicitly designed to capture long-term dependencies in non-linear time-series data. LSTMs model sequence learning (price/volume history) by utilizing "forget gates" to solve the vanishing gradient problem.
2. **Algorithmic Sentiment Analysis (NLP):** Transformer-based natural language processing models (e.g., FinBERT) that ingest millions of unstructured text data points (news, SEC filings, social media) to output quantitative sentiment scores, successfully predicting volatility clustering and directional momentum.
3. **Hidden Markov Models (HMM):** Statistical probability models used to identify unobservable market "regimes" (e.g., high-volatility bear, low-volatility bull) based on observable emissions like daily returns and variance.
4. **GARCH (Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity):** An econometric model used to estimate and forecast time-varying market volatility, operating on the premise that variance is dependent on past variances (volatility clustering).

### Atom 4: Fundamental-Technical Intersection
Fundamental growth metrics manifest in technical chart patterns just prior to a breakout through mechanical valuation pressures. When a company exhibits accelerating EPS growth but the stock price remains flat, a **"value divergence"** occurs. The P/E ratio mechanically compresses, making the asset increasingly attractive to value-driven quant algorithms. Technically, this lack of selling pressure combined with quiet accumulation manifests as Volatility Contraction Patterns (VCP) or tightening Bollinger Bands. Similarly, consistent dividend history creates **"Support Hardening."** Institutional yield-seekers programmatically layer buy limit orders at price levels where the dividend yield hits a target threshold (e.g., 8%), turning major technical moving averages into absolute price floors. Pre-breakout, these assets typically form ascending triangles, as steady intrinsic growth continually raises the "higher lows" of the pattern until seller exhaustion triggers the breakout.

### Atom 5: The "Noise" Factor
Several globally accepted indicators completely fail or generate catastrophic false signals depending on market microstructure. Momentum oscillators, particularly the Relative Strength Index (RSI) and Stochastic oscillators, are highly susceptible to "whipsawing." In illiquid environments, a single anomalous block trade can spike the RSI to overbought (>80) instantly, triggering a false sell signal despite no fundamental change. Similarly, fast moving-average crossovers (e.g., the 5-EMA crossing the 13-EMA) generate extreme noise; wide bid-ask spreads artificially distort the daily closing price, causing the MAs to cross back and forth aimlessly. Standard high-low breakout strategies (like Donchian Channels) frequently result in "bull traps," as low liquidity allows minor capital to briefly push prices past resistance, only to collapse when no follow-through volume exists.

## PHASE 2: SYNTHESIS & CONTRACTION

### Adapting Global Quantitative Models to NEPSE Constraints *(Atom 1 + Atom 3)*
To successfully deploy global forecasting models in NEPSE, a researcher must fundamentally alter the mathematics to account for local microstructure constraints.

* **Constrained LSTMs:** Standard LSTMs will fail in NEPSE because they linearly extrapolate momentum. The LSTM architecture must be modified by embedding the SEBON ±10% daily circuit breaker into the loss function as a hard non-linear activation limit. The model must "know" that a price mathematically cannot exceed this bound, preventing it from predicting impossible exponential curves. Furthermore, a new feature layer for T+2 settlement lag must be added, as localized illiquidity clusters form precisely two days after heavy volume distribution.
* **Localized NLP Sentiment Models:** A global model like FinBERT is useless in Nepal. The transformer model must be heavily retrained on a custom lexicon of Romanized Nepali (*"badhchha"*, *"ghatchha"*, *"kheladi"*), absorbing data strictly from retail hubs like ShareSansar, NepseAlpha, and local Clubhouse rooms. Because NEPSE is retail-dominated, institutional news releases must be down-weighted, and retail social sentiment must be heavily up-weighted in the NLP attention mechanism.
* **Asymmetrical EGARCH:** Because NEPSE is strictly long-only, standard GARCH models will miscalculate downside risk. A researcher must utilize Exponential GARCH (EGARCH) to model asymmetric shocks. In standard markets, negative news drives higher volatility (panic selling); in NEPSE, positive shocks (syndicate accumulation or "cornering") often generate *more* volatility than negative shocks due to circuit-breaker FOMO buying by retail herds.

### The NEPSE "Growth Checklist" *(Atom 2 + Atom 4)*
By fusing pure local price action with the fundamental-technical intersection, we yield a highly actionable, NEPSE-specific screening checklist for detecting business growth pre-breakout:

1. **The VCP-EPS Anomaly:** Track the rolling P/E ratio over 3 quarters. Is the P/E actively contracting due to rising EPS, while the stock chart simultaneously exhibits a Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP) with volume dropping by >50% of the 50-day average? *This is the ultimate signature of smart money hiding their tracks before earnings.*
2. **Dividend Yield Support Hardening:** Identify the exact price level where the company's historical average dividend payout equates to a >7% yield. Does this price intersect exactly with a long-term technical floor (e.g., 200-day SMA or a multi-year W-bottom)? *If so, downside risk is mathematically neutralized.*
3. **The Nepalese Quarter-End Volume Anomaly:** Has the stock experienced a sudden volume spike (>300% of the 20-day SMA) that occurred specifically 10 to 15 days prior to the close of Ashad, Ashwin, Poush, or Chaitra, followed by immediate price stabilization? *This indicates illegal or gray-market information leakage regarding upcoming quarterly reports.*
4. **The "Locked Float" Pre-Open Tell:** Is the daily volume near zero, yet the stock opens higher via pre-open session manipulation? *This indicates the tradable float has been successfully cornered, and fundamental growth is being weaponized to gap the stock up.*

### Strict Warning Guide *(Based on Atom 5)*
To survive the NEPSE ecosystem, researchers and quants must strictly discard the following "noise":

* **Discard Standard RSI on Low-Cap Stocks:** Never use RSI or Stochastic oscillators on low-cap Hydropower or Microfinance sectors. The bid-ask spread and low float allow a single retail trader with minimal capital to print an RSI of 85, triggering a false quant sell signal.
* **Ban Moving Average Crossovers:** Exclude fast MA crossovers (e.g., 5/13 EMA) from your trading algorithms entirely. The T+2 settlement cycle and fragmentation of capital guarantee chronic whipsaw losses in NEPSE.
* **Beware the Retail Bull Trap:** Do not program algorithms to buy standard resistance breakouts (like 52-week highs) unless they are backed by the localized quarter-end volume anomaly. In a long-only market lacking institutional liquidity to sustain a push, generic chart breakouts fail over 60% of the time, leaving automated systems holding peak-price bags.
